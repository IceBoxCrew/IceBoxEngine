# Code of Conduct

IceBox Engine is a commercial, proprietary 2D game engine made by IceBoxCrew
Studio. Its source is not public. What is public is a GitHub repository, an email
address, and the people who turn up in them — licensees reporting bugs, people
deciding whether to buy, security researchers, and us.

This document is about how everyone behaves in those spaces. It is not the
license and it is not a support agreement. It does not change what you may do
with software you have paid for: [LICENSE.txt](LICENSE.txt) governs that, and
nothing here overrides it.

*Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-08-16. This document is published in both of the
project's repositories and applies identically in each.*

---

## The short version

- **Aim criticism at the work, not at the person.** Blunt is fine. Contemptuous
  is not.
- **This applies to us too**, on the same terms, including whoever owns the
  repositories.
- **Report a problem by email** — [iceboxcrew057@gmail.com](mailto:iceboxcrew057@gmail.com)
  with `CONDUCT` in the subject. English or Russian.
- **Being critical of the engine is not a violation.** A bad review, an argument
  that a design decision is wrong, a complaint about the price — we will not hide,
  lock or ban over any of it.
- **Nothing here can touch your license.** No sanction under this document
  revokes a Key, ends a license, stops your updates, or affects a game you have
  shipped.

This box is a summary. The sections below are the document.

## Who this applies to

Everyone in the spaces listed below, on the same terms:

- **Us** — IceBoxCrew Studio, including whoever owns the repositories. A code of
  conduct that binds only the people who did not write it is worth nothing.
- **You** — whether you hold a license, are still deciding, are here to report a
  security issue, or simply wandered in.
- **Anyone we give access** to our private development repository —
  collaborators, contractors, testers.

| Space | What happens there |
| ----- | ------------------ |
| **The public repository** — [IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine](https://github.com/IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine) | [Issues](https://github.com/IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine/issues), [Discussions](https://github.com/IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine/discussions) (announcements, questions, ideas, show-and-tell), documentation pull requests, security advisories, the README and the documentation |

| **Anywhere someone speaks for the project** | Using our name, our logo or an official account to represent IceBox Engine |

What you do elsewhere is not our business — until it is aimed at someone because
of these spaces. Harassment that starts in an issue and continues by DM, on
another platform, or in a storefront review is still harassment we will act on.
Conversely, we do not police your own game's community, your Discord, or an
argument you had somewhere unrelated to IceBox Engine.

of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal

We pledge to behave in ways that keep this a welcoming and technically honest
place to be.

## What we expect

- **Say the thing plainly.** "This API is confusing and here is why" is useful.
  "Whoever wrote this is an idiot" is not, and it is not more true for being
  louder.
- **Assume the other person has less context than you, not less intelligence.**
  Most bad reports are missing information, not malice.
- **Give enough to act on.** Version, platform, GPU, steps — what the
  [issue templates](https://github.com/IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine/issues/new/choose)
  ask for. A minimal project that reproduces the bug is worth more than a
  paragraph of adjectives.
- **Take people at their word about themselves.** If someone tells you their
  name, their pronouns or how they want to be addressed, use it. There is nothing
  to debate there.
- **Accept a decision you disagree with.** You can argue your case once, well. A
  request we decline, or an issue we close, is not an invitation to reopen the
  argument in five other threads.
- **Give credit.** If someone's repro, script or answer solved your problem, say
  so.
- **Keep other people's material out of it.** Do not paste code, assets,
  documentation or internal correspondence you have no right to share —
  including material from another engine, from an employer, or under an NDA.
- **Respect what is not published yet.** If we give you access to a pre-release
  build, an unreleased feature or the development repository, treat it as
  confidential until we publish it, unless we tell you otherwise.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment in any form: unwanted sexual attention or advances, sexualized
  language or imagery, sustained personal attacks, deliberate misgendering,
  insults aimed at who someone is, or continued contact after being asked to
  stop.
- Any sexual content involving minors. This is reported to GitHub and, where
  appropriate, to the authorities, and it is the end of your participation here.
- Threats, incitement to violence, wishing harm on someone, or encouraging
  self-harm.
- Doxxing: publishing anyone's real name, address, employer, email, IP address,
  photograph or other private information without their clear permission —
  including details dug out of a crash log, a screenshot, a project file or a
  commit.
- Stalking, brigading, or organizing a pile-on.
- Slurs, discriminatory language, or "jokes" about the characteristics listed in
  the pledge.
- Trolling, deliberate derailing, or sustained disruption of a thread, the issue
  tracker or the discussions.
- Impersonating IceBoxCrew Studio or anyone in it, or using our name or logo to
  suggest that we endorse, sponsor or are affiliated with your product — see
  [LICENSE.txt](LICENSE.txt) Section 14.3.
- Spam and off-topic promotion: advertising, referral links, mass identical
  posts, or using Discussions as a marketplace. Showing what you built with the
  engine is not spam — show-and-tell is there for exactly that.
- Asking for, offering or linking to cracked builds, License Keys, key
  generators, or ways around activation. Beyond being unwelcome here, that is a
  license matter under [LICENSE.txt](LICENSE.txt) Sections 7(e), 7(f) and 9.5,
  handled separately from this document.
- Knowingly false reports, reports filed to harass someone, and floods of
  unverified machine-generated issues. Use whatever tools help you write —
  including AI — but read what you send before you send it; a very small studio
  has no capacity to spare on reports nobody checked.
- Publishing private correspondence with us, or someone else's report, without
  permission.
- Retaliating against anyone for making a report.

gets it fixed faster:

| A bug, a crash, a missing feature | [Issues](https://github.com/IceBoxCrew/IceBoxEngine/issues) |

And to be explicit, because it is worth being explicit: a technical disagreement,
a feature request we decline, an issue closed as won't-fix, a review of the
engine we did not enjoy reading, or someone being blunt about a bug are **not**
violations of this document. Being wrong is not misconduct, and neither is being
unimpressed.

## Reporting

**Email [iceboxcrew057@gmail.com](mailto:iceboxcrew057@gmail.com) with `CONDUCT`
in the subject line.** That is the whole procedure — there is no form.

Please do not use the security advisory channel for this; it is for
vulnerabilities and is read on a different schedule. Please do not open a public
issue either: it puts the person you are reporting on a stage, and it puts you
there beside them.

Write in **English or Russian** — whichever you can be precise in. Both reach us.

Useful, if you have it, but send the report even if you have none of it:

- Links to the comments, issues or discussions involved.
- Screenshots, in case something is edited or deleted afterwards.
- Whether it is still going on.
- What you would like to happen. "Just make it stop" is a valid answer, and so is
  "I want nothing done, I want it on the record."

You can also report content to GitHub directly at
[github.com/contact/report-abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse). That
channel does not depend on us, which is precisely why it is worth knowing about.

### If the report is about us

IceBoxCrew Studio is very small, and the mailbox above is read by the people who
make the engine. If your report concerns one of us, say so in the first line.
Where there is someone else in the studio to handle it, they will, and the person
it concerns will not see it. Where there is not, we will tell you that honestly
rather than pretend to a wall that does not exist — and in that case GitHub's
abuse channel above is the escalation that does not rely on our good faith.

### What we do with a report

- We read it and act on it ourselves. It is not forwarded outside the studio and
  it is not published.
- We do not tell the person you reported who reported them, unless you ask us to.
  Sometimes the content makes it obvious; where we think it will, we say so
  before we act.
- We keep reports for as long as we need them to act and to recognize a repeat
  pattern later.
- [PRIVACY_NOTICE.txt](PRIVACY_NOTICE.txt) covers crash reports and license
  activation only — it does not cover this correspondence. If you are in the EEA
  or the UK and want to know what we hold about a report, ask at the same
  address.
- **Retaliating against someone for reporting is itself a violation**, and is
  treated at least as seriously as whatever was reported.

## What happens next

IceBoxCrew Studio is a very small studio, so — as in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) —
these are honest targets rather than a service level we are selling you:

| Stage | Target |
| ----- | ------ |
| Acknowledge that your report arrived | within **3 working days** |
| Tell you what we decided and what we are doing | within **14 days** |
| Act on something urgent — a threat, doxxing, an ongoing pile-on | the same day we see it, before the investigation is finished |

If the acknowledgement window passes in silence, send it again. A report that
never arrived, or that landed in a spam folder, is far likelier than a decision
to ignore you.

### Enforcement

| Level | What it means in practice | When |
| ----- | ------------------------- | ---- |
| **1 — A word in private** | An email saying what was wrong and what we expect instead. Nothing public, nothing held against you afterwards. | A first lapse, or something that read worse than it was meant. |
| **2 — A formal warning** | The same in writing, with a stated condition for continuing to take part. A comment may be hidden, a thread may be locked. | A repeat after level 1, or a single clearly deliberate incident. |
| **3 — Temporary removal** | Blocked from the repositories, their issues and their discussions for a stated period, with a date on which it ends. Email to us stays open — including for security reports. | A serious incident, or a pattern that did not stop after level 2. |
| **4 — Permanent removal** | Blocked indefinitely from every space in this document, and reported to GitHub where their terms are engaged. | Harassment of a person, threats, doxxing, or any single act severe enough that a temporary ban would be a formality. |

We may skip levels. A credible threat does not get a polite first email. Some
things are acted on before any of this — threats of violence, doxxing, sexualized
content involving minors, or an account that exists only to abuse people: we
remove first and explain afterwards.

Whatever we do, we say in writing which level we applied and why — afterwards,
in the cases where we had to act first. We do not sanction people silently.

### What enforcement never touches

You bought a tool. An argument in a GitHub thread does not take it away from you.
A sanction under this document does not, and cannot:

- **Revoke or deactivate a License Key.** [LICENSE.txt](LICENSE.txt) Section 9.7
  lists the only grounds on which a Key may be revoked and states that we will
  not revoke one for any other reason. Conduct is not among them.
- **End your license, or stop your updates.** Every future update, free, across
  major versions, is a term of the license (Section 11.2) — not a reward for
  behavior.
- **Take away anything else the license grants you.** You may go on using the
  engine, go on shipping and selling the games you make with it, and go on
  writing, publishing and selling your own plugins and mods (Sections 4, 5 and
  6).
- **Affect a game you have shipped**, your players, your revenue, or your
  ownership of anything you made (Sections 5, 8.2 and 17.4).
- **Bar you from reporting a security issue.** Someone blocked from the
  repositories can still email a vulnerability; we will still fix it and still
  credit them, on the terms in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
- **Remove your access to the documentation, the installer or your projects.**

The one thing that runs the other way: where the behavior is itself a breach of
the license — publishing or reselling the engine, sharing your Key, defeating
activation — the license has its own consequences, in Sections 9.7 and 17.3, and
those apply on their own terms. The two are decided separately, and we will tell
you which one you are hearing about.

### Appeals

If you think a decision was wrong, reply to it, or write to
[iceboxcrew057@gmail.com](mailto:iceboxcrew057@gmail.com) with `CONDUCT APPEAL`
in the subject, within **30 days**. Tell us what we got wrong — new information
helps more than a restatement of the original argument.

Where the studio has someone other than the original decision-maker to review it,
they will, and we will say plainly which was the case. One appeal per decision,
answered within **14 days**. We either lift it, shorten it, or explain why it
stands.

## If you send us work or ideas

The engine's source is not public, so there is no code here to send a patch
against. The public repository does carry the documentation, the README and the
issue templates, and pull requests to those are welcome — corrections, clearer
examples and translations especially.

Because the project is proprietary, a few things are better said plainly than
left vague:

- **It has to be yours to give.** Do not send text, code, art or documentation
  that belongs to someone else, is lifted from another engine or its docs, or
  that you are not free to hand over.
- **Sending it grants us the right to use it.** If we use what you send, you
  grant IceBoxCrew Studio a worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free right
  to use, modify, publish and distribute it as part of IceBox Engine and its
  documentation, including in the commercial product. You keep your own copyright
  — you are licensing it to us, not giving it away.
- **There is no payment unless we agreed one in writing beforehand.** Send work
  on that understanding, or do not send it.
- **We are not obliged to accept it.** We may decline without giving a reason,
  take a long time, or solve the same problem differently later.
- **Ideas and feature requests create no rights and no obligation.** If you
  describe a feature in an issue or a discussion, we may build it, and we owe you
  nothing for it. We will not claim you wrote something you did not — if you want
  crediting for a contribution, ask, and we will.
- **This is not a contributor license agreement.** If a contribution is ever
  large enough to need one, we will say so and ask you to sign one first.

## What you can expect from us

- **Criticism is not misconduct.** A bad review, a complaint about the price, an
  argument that a design decision is wrong — none of that is a violation, and we
  will not hide, lock, delete or ban over it. We hide and lock for the behavior
  described above, and when we do, we say that we did and why.
- **We do not rewrite what you said.** We may hide a comment; we will not quietly
  edit one and leave your name on it.
- **We answer, eventually.** We are small enough that "eventually" is sometimes
  the honest word, and support is a courtesy rather than a contractual response
  time ([LICENSE.txt](LICENSE.txt) Section 11.3). Ask again rather than assume
  contempt.
- **This applies to us.** If we get it wrong, say so — in the thread, or by email
  if you would rather not do it in public.
- **We will not use this document to win an argument.** It exists to keep people
  safe, not to end a debate that is going badly for us.
