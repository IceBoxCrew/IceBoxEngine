================================================================================
                            IceBoxEngine - CHANGELOG
================================================================================

Version:           Release 1.0.2 (R-1.0.2)
Previous release:  Release 1.0.1 (R-1.0.1)

This file lists what changed in this version of IceBoxEngine since the previous
release: what was added, what was improved and what was fixed in the editor,
the launcher, the updater and the runtime your games are built with. Every
feature named here is described in full in the documentation - the
Documentation folder next to this file, which you can also read in the editor
under Help > Documentation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

:Added:

- Profiler: CPU and GPU temperatures on macOS - from the SMC on Intel Macs, and
  from the SMC and the IOHID thermal sensors on Apple silicon.
- Profiler: CPU and GPU temperatures on Android - from the kernel thermal zones
  and, on Adreno GPUs, from kgsl - on devices whose SELinux policy lets apps
  read them.
- Profiler: Thermal Status and Thermal Load on Android - how hard the system is
  throttling the device to keep it cool - with a Thermal Load graph. Both are
  also written into recorded traces.
- Lua API: Settings.GetThermalStatus(), Settings.GetThermalStatusName(),
  Settings.IsThermalStatusAvailable(), Settings.GetThermalLoad(),
  Settings.IsThermalLoadAvailable() and the Settings.THERMAL_* constants, so a
  game can lower its own quality before Android starts throttling the device.
  Settings.OnSettingChanged reports status changes with the key "ThermalStatus".
  The five functions are also available as Visual Script nodes.
- Profiler: Core Temp and MSI Afterburner are read as temperature sources on
  Windows while they run, with nothing to configure.
- Profiler: hovering a temperature that shows N/A explains why it is
  unavailable on your platform and, where possible, how to make it available.

:Improved:

- Temperature sensors: a hardware monitor started while the editor is already
  open is picked up within seconds, without a restart, and a source that stops
  answering is dropped.
- Temperature sensors: the profiler names the source and the exact sensor it
  reads - for example "LibreHardwareMonitor: CPU Package" or
  "hwmon: k10temp Tctl" - and shows the hottest one when several qualify. At
  startup the log names the source of each temperature, or why there is none.
- Profiler: Chrome-trace exports now carry CPU and GPU temperature tracks and,
  on Android, Thermal Status and Thermal Load tracks.
- AI Helper plugin: its version numbering starts over at 1.0.0 (it showed 3.1.0
  before); the plugin itself is unchanged.

:Fixed:

- Launcher and editor text fields: clicking an item of the right-click menu
  (Undo, Redo, Cut, Copy, Paste, Delete, Select All) with the mouse did
  nothing. The item now runs, and the menu closes, when the left or the right
  mouse button is released over it.
- Level changes: LoadLevel, ReloadLevel, ApplyAsyncLevel and
  LoadLevelAsync(path, true) could show the new level for one frame in its raw,
  as-saved state. The level is now swapped right after the current frame is
  shown - so a fade to black that ends with LoadLevel really reaches black -
  and the new level is first drawn only after its first full update, scripts,
  widgets and camera included, with its World Settings already applied.
- Level start: the first update of a new level - after a level change or when
  Play starts in the editor - advanced by the length of the frame that loaded
  it, so fade-ins and intro animations jumped ahead. It now reuses the delta of
  the frame before.
- Camera lag: when a level started, the camera slid in from its previous
  position. The view now starts exactly at the camera's target, and lag only
  smooths the movement after that.
- Adaptive Quality: loading a level could be mistaken for a frame-rate drop and
  lower the quality. The frame that starts a level and the level's first frame
  are now ignored.
- Widgets: a widget that loaded in the middle of a frame - for example one
  shown from a button callback - could be drawn once in its as-saved state. In
  Play a widget is now drawn only after OnInit and its first update, and the
  widgets in its visible SubWidget elements load and update together with it.
  A cinema's Custom Widget dialogue follows the same rule.
- Editor: a level change that a script had requested but that had not happened
  yet (LoadLevel, ReloadLevel, LoadLevelAsync) carried over into the next Play
  session. Leaving Play now drops it.
- Touch screens: a UI element stayed hovered after the finger lifted, keeping
  its hover color, so it looked selected. The hover now ends, with OnUnhover,
  when the touch ends.
- Video: picture and sound could drift apart after long frames or loading
  hitches. The picture now follows the video's own sound, and a sound track
  that starts later than the picture or has a gap is played at its place. On
  Web and iOS the browser and AVFoundation keep them in sync, as before.
- Video: videos kept playing - picture and sound - while the game was paused,
  stopped at a Lua debugger breakpoint or suspended. Every video channel now
  pauses with the game and resumes in sync from the same moment.
- Networking: Connect, JoinOnline, HostOnline or StartServer called while an
  automatic reconnect was still pending could fail. The pending reconnect is
  now abandoned and the new session starts.
- Android: with Is Suspended on, an active network session - hosting,
  connected, connecting or reconnecting - froze while the app was in the
  background. It now keeps running, with nothing drawn until the app returns,
  and Settings.SetIsSuspended takes effect at once instead of on the next
  launch.
- Metal and Vulkan (MoltenVK) on macOS and iOS: shadows and light cookies used
  texture slots beyond the 16 samplers Apple GPUs provide per shader stage, so
  they could render incorrectly.
- Windows: without a hardware monitor running, the CPU temperature could be
  read from a battery, charger or other thermal zone that is not the
  processor. Such zones are now ignored.
- Laptops with two GPUs: reading the GPU temperature could wake the sleeping
  dedicated GPU and report a GPU the editor was not rendering on. Only the GPU
  the editor renders on is read now.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This file is rewritten for every new version of IceBoxEngine. It always lists
only the changes of the version it ships with; the changes of earlier versions
are not kept here.
